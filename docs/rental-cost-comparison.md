## 3. ⚠️ Hidden fees

Both listings understate. SoHo's omissions are larger and **confirmed**; Petra Nera's are
smaller but **unverified** — which makes them riskier.

### ⚠️ Petra Nera — two things to press on

**1. The R2,000 admin fee is high.** Market rate for a lease initiation fee is R950–R1,500,
so this is roughly R500–R1,000 above normal, and non-refundable. Ask what it covers and
whether it's negotiable.

**2. The utilities came in at R1,150, not R800** — R1,000 electricity and R150 water. That
is R350/month more than first quoted, and it checks out: a three-bed heating its own water
at City Power's 2026/27 rates should land near R1,000, and it is exactly R300 above SoHo's

R1,330 to R980.**

**3. Sanitation and refuse are still unaccounted for.** SoHo bills these separately at R900
and R500. If Petra Nera does the same, that is up to **R1,400/month** on top of the R1,150 —
which would make SoHo Junction the cheaper flat outright. This is now the single most
valuable question left to ask, worth more than the rent difference, the gym and the Uber
fares combined.

## 6. Method — how the utility figures were checked

### Water — R50 confirmed ✅

Johannesburg Water gives the **first 6 kL a month free** to residential customers, then
charges R16.45/kL from 6 to 10 kL. Two people typically use 6–8 kL, so the metered cost
really is in the tens of rands. **R50 is right** — the earlier R350 estimate was wrong.
Watch only for the demand-management levy, which rose to about R108/month in July 2026 and
may sit on the body-corporate account rather than yours.

### Electricity — R120 is the fixed charge, not the bill

City Power's 2026/27 prepaid residential tariff runs **R3.06–R4.37 per kWh**, plus roughly
R200/month in fixed charges — a R70 service charge and a **R130 network capacity charge**.
That R130 is almost certainly the R120 figure you had in mind; actual consumption sits on
top of it.

A two-person flat uses 300–450 kWh/month. Strip out the geyser at 30–40% and SoHo lands
around **200–280 kWh, or R650–R900**. R700 is used in this model. **Petra Nera's actual
R1,000** — a larger flat, heating its own water — sits almost exactly where this model
predicts, which is good evidence the R700 estimate for SoHo is sound. Still worth asking
the agent for a current tenant's real number.

## 8. Stress test — what would flip this

The R980 gap is **thin**. One unresolved question and one lifestyle choice can each close
it on their own — and either would make SoHo Junction the cheaper flat.

| If… | B monthly | Gap | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Base case** — utilities R1,150, gym 3×/week | R12,793 | R980 | Petra Nera ahead |
| SoHo's electricity is R900 rather than R700 | R12,793 | R1,180 *(A rises to R13,973)* | Petra Nera further ahead |
| You go to gym twice a week rather than three times | R12,403 | R1,370 | Petra Nera furthest ahead |
| Petra Nera bills sanitation and refuse on top, at R700 | R13,493 | R280 | **Effectively a tie** |
| You go to gym 5.5 times a week | ~R13,770 | ~R0 | **Dead even** |
| You go to gym six times a week | R13,963 | **−R190** | **SoHo cheaper** |
| Petra Nera bills sanitation and refuse at SoHo's level, R1,400 | R14,193 | **−R420** | **SoHo cheaper** |

**One question decides this.** The utilities are now settled at R1,150. What is not settled
is whether Petra Nera bills **sanitation and refuse on top**, as SoHo does at R900 and R500.
Anything above roughly **R980/month** on that line and SoHo Junction becomes the cheaper
flat. Ask this before anything else — it outweighs the rent, the gym and every fare here
combined.

## 10. Verdict

**Petra Nera — on a margin now thin enough to be uncomfortable.**

Petra Nera is R980/month cheaper — R490 each. It gives the two of you a third room to use
as a study and removes the church commute entirely. On the numbers as they now stand, it
still wins.

But the margin has halved twice. It was R1,672 on a private car with the original utility
quote; R1,330 once Uber replaced the car; and **R980 now that Petra Nera's real utility
bill came in R350 higher**. Two forces did that. The gym at Petra Nera costs
**R1,820/month** once Uber is counted, which cancels almost all of the walking-distance
saving. And Petra Nera pays for its own geyser where SoHo does not.

R980 is roughly the value of *one* unanswered question. If sanitation and refuse are billed
on top at Petra Nera the way they are at SoHo, the decision reverses.

### Three questions decide it

1. **Does Petra Nera bill sanitation and refuse on top of the R1,150?** SoHo charges R900
   and R500 for exactly these. Anything above R980 on that line and SoHo Junction is the
   cheaper flat. Get it in writing before anything else — this single answer outweighs the
   rent, the gym and every fare in this document.
2. **How seriously will you train?** At two visits a week Petra Nera is R1,370 ahead. At
   5.5 the two are dead even; at six, SoHo is cheaper. That crossover used to be out of
   reach — it no longer is. If either of you trains properly, SoHo's gym is not a perk,
   it's a line item worth R1,820/month.
3. **Is R490 each enough to buy the flat you like less?** That's what the decision now
   costs. R490/month is real money, but it's no longer the kind of gap that settles an
   argument on its own — and Petra Nera is the plainer place to come home to. Set against
   it: nothing stands between you and church. No booking, no fare, no waiting. That's the
   strongest thing Petra Nera has, and it isn't on this spreadsheet.

**Recommendation:** Petra Nera — conditional on confirming in writing that sanitation and
refuse are *not* billed on top, seeing the security setup in person, and walking the
Riverbend Road route to CRC after dark yourselves. But this is now a close call rather than
a clear one. If that levy question comes back at more than about R980, or if you both
intend to train five or six days a week, **choose SoHo Junction** — on those numbers it is
both cheaper and materially better to live in.

## 11. Questions to ask before signing

### SoHo Junction

1. Are the R900 sanitation and R500 refuse/security fixed, or do they escalate annually?
2. What is a realistic monthly electricity spend for a two-bed here, given the central geyser? Ask for a current tenant's actual figure.
3. Are both parking bays included at no extra charge, and allocated in the lease?
4. What is the annual escalation percentage, and does it apply to the levies too?
5. Are any special levies planned? Can we see the last two AGM minutes and the reserve-fund balance?
6. What are the Activity Centre, gym and pool hours, and is there any additional access charge?
7. Is the business centre bookable, and is there a fee?
8. What is the deposit interest arrangement, and which account is it held in?
9. What is the total once-off cost — lease admin, credit checks for two, access tags, meter activation?
10. Does the complex have a generator or inverter for load-shedding, and water tanks for Joburg Water outages?
11. What is the early-cancellation penalty, and can it be capped in writing?
12. Is the R10,000 deposit confirmed for the unfurnished option?

### Petra Nera

1. **Are sanitation, sewerage and refuse billed on top of the R1,150, or included?** This is the decisive question — SoHo charges R1,400 for these.
2. If they are billed separately, what did they come to last month?
3. Can we see a recent utility bill or statement for the unit, so the R1,150 is confirmed rather than estimated?
4. **What does the R2,000 admin fee cover, and is it negotiable?** Market rate is R950–R1,500.
5. What is the annual escalation percentage?
6. What is the security setup — guard, access control, CCTV, perimeter?
7. How many parking bays, and are they covered or allocated?
8. Backup power and water storage during load-shedding and municipal outages?
9. Age and condition of the geyser, plumbing and roof — and who pays for repairs?
10. Will the landlord contribute to repainting or basic cosmetic improvement given the condition?
11. What is the exact walking route and distance to the CRC entrance, and is it lit at night?
12. When is it available, and is the R9,000 held for a 12-month lease?
