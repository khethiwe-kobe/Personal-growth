Verdict

Two real contenders, and a third if flexibility matters more than money

Petra Nera and the R8,800 SoHo unit are R280 a month apart — R140 each. That is no longer a financial decision. Meander costs about R19,000 more over a year, and buys flexibility rather than value.

The gap has closed steadily: R1,672 when this started, R1,330 once Uber replaced the car, R980 when Petra Nera’s real utility bill arrived, and R280 now that the cheaper SoHo unit is on the table. It is fragile in one direction only — five of eight stress-test scenarios make SoHo cheaper, while the ones favouring Petra Nera need you to use the gym less.

So the live choice is a straight trade, and both sides are real: a third bedroom and a six-minute walk to church, against a gym, a pool, a guard house and a flat you would rather come home to. Meander only wins if what you need is a short lease and low move-in cash.

Four questions decide it

Q1

How big is the R8,800 SoHo unit? The R9,500 flat is 62 m²; this one does not say. If it is materially smaller the R700 is not a saving, and Petra Nera’s three bedrooms pull ahead. Quick to ask, and it could end the comparison.

Q2

Does Petra Nera bill sanitation and refuse on top of the R1,150? SoHo charges R1,400 for exactly these. Anything above R280 and SoHo is cheaper outright — a low bar to clear.

Q3

Will you train more than three times a week? At 3.7 visits the two are level; past that SoHo is cheaper and has the gym downstairs.

Q4

Do you need to be able to leave? If there is a real chance you move within twelve months, Meander’s month-to-month lease and zero deposit are worth more than the R1,405 a month it costs over SoHo. If you are settling for a year or more, they are not.

Recommendation: the R8,800 SoHo unit, unless the floor size comes back materially below 62 m² or the third bedroom genuinely matters more than the amenities. It is R700 a month below the unit you first looked at, available now, cheapest of the Fourways options to move into, and it costs R140 each a month more than Petra Nera for a gym, a pool and a manned guard house. Move quickly — it was available from 1 August.

Choose Petra Nera if the third room is genuinely load-bearing, or if walking to church matters more than any of this. Choose Meander if you cannot or do not want to commit to twelve months, or if move-in cash is the binding constraint — but price the two Uber trips first, because they are what put it last.

Method

How the figures were checked

Water

Johannesburg Water gives the first 6 kL a month free to residential customers, then R16.45/kL from 6 to 10 kL. Two people typically use 6–8 kL, so the metered cost really is in the tens of rands. Watch for the demand-management levy, which rose to about R108 a month in July 2026 and may sit on the body-corporate account.

Electricity — the figures corroborate

City Power’s 2026/27 prepaid tariff runs R3.06–R4.37 per kWh plus about R200 a month fixed — a R70 service charge and a R130 network capacity charge. A two-person flat uses 300–450 kWh; strip the geyser at 30–40% and SoHo lands at 200–280 kWh, or R650–R900. Petra Nera’s actual R1,000 — larger, heating its own water — sits where this model predicts, which is good evidence the estimates are sound.

Uber fares

Your two figures — R50 over about 5 km, R95 over about 25 km — fit a fare of roughly R39 plus R2.25 a kilometre. That model produces the Meander and gym fares. It is the weakest link in the comparison and the reason Meander lands last, so price those trips in the app before relying on it.

Internet — new this round

Uncapped home fibre runs R600–R900 a month in 2026; R800 is used for the three options that do not include it. This raised every total by R800 but left the gaps between Petra Nera and the SoHo units unchanged.
