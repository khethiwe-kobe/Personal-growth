Fitness & wellness

Replacing just the gym externally costs R650 a month, plus fuel, plus roughly twenty minutes of round-trip travel per session. The real value is the near-zero friction — which is the actual reason most memberships lapse. A gym you walk downstairs to at 05:30 gets used. One that needs a car at 05:30 often doesn’t.

Work & study

The business centre is a genuine asset for Wits work — a quiet, powered, seated workspace outside the flat, on the property, at no extra cost. The alternative is coffee shops at R60–R100 a sitting, roughly R800 a month at twice a week. The function room adds hosting capacity without needing a large flat or paid venue hire.

Security & comfort

Two parking bays included, one covered — many Fourways complexes charge R250–R500 a month for a second. North-facing means better light and a lower winter heating load. And the 24/7 guard matters on the Wednesday return from Braamfontein.

The quiet saving

Water heating is typically 30–40% of a household electricity bill. Having the geyser on the communal account is an ongoing R250–R400 a month back in your pocket, and it is why the R700 electricity estimate above is lower than you would expect for a two-bedroom.

Verify this first

For a three-bedroom unit, R800 covering both electricity and water is low. It very likely excludes sanitation and refuse — which at SoHo are billed separately at R1,400. Ask explicitly: “Is R800 the total, or does the body corporate bill sanitation, sewerage and refuse on top?” If R700–R1,000 is hiding there, the gap between these two options narrows sharply. This is the single most important unknown in the whole comparison.

Three ways to read the gap

Value angles worth thinking about

Cost per bedroom

42%

SoHo Junction works out to R6,396 a bedroom; Petra Nera to R3,707. Petra Nera is 42% cheaper per bedroom — and it is the bigger place. At SoHo you are paying more for less space.

The third bedroom is an asset

R62,000

Let the third room at a modest R3,500 a month and effective housing cost drops to about R7,620 — roughly R5,170 a month below SoHo Junction, or R62,000 a year. Even used purely as a Wits study, it substitutes for the business centre SoHo charges a premium for. Three-bedroom units are scarce here, so the option itself carries value.

Break-even

R7,828

For SoHo Junction to cost the same as Petra Nera, its rent would have to drop to R7,828 — a R1,672 reduction. Read the other way, Petra Nera could absorb a rent increase to R10,672 before the two are equal. That is your negotiating headroom.

Verdict

Petra Nera, conditional on three checks

On pure numbers, Petra Nera wins clearly and by a wide margin — R1,672 a month cheaper, an extra bedroom, and the church commute eliminated entirely.

Over the lease that is roughly R20,000; if the third bedroom is let, closer to R62,000. But SoHo Junction is not overpriced — the amenity bundle is genuinely worth about what you would pay for it. You are buying a lifestyle package rather than accommodation, and it comes with one less bedroom and a commute to the place you go most often.

It reduces to three honest questions

Q1

Will you actually use the pool, sauna, steam room and business centre — not just the gym? If yes, SoHo’s premium is fair value. If it is really just the gym, you are paying R1,672 a month for something worth R650 to you, and giving up a bedroom to get it.

Q2

Is the R800 at Petra Nera really all-in? If sanitation and refuse are billed on top, the gap shrinks to R700–R1,000 a month and SoHo becomes much more competitive.

Q3

How much is walking to church worth to you? Not in rands — in participation. Three round trips a week, every week, for a year is 169 km and 6.5 hours a month of friction between you and something central to your life. That is the argument a spreadsheet cannot score.

Recommendation: Petra Nera — conditional on confirming the R800 covers sanitation and refuse, seeing the security setup in person, and walking the Riverbend Road route to CRC after dark yourself. If any of those three fail, the options are close enough that SoHo Junction’s amenities and presentation justify the difference.

Before signing

Questions to ask each landlord

SoHo Junction
  1. Is the R900 sanitation and R500 refuse/security fixed, or does it escalate annually?
  2. Are both parking bays included at no extra charge, and are they allocated in the lease?
  3. What is the annual escalation percentage, and does it apply to the levies too?
  4. Are any special levies planned? Can I see the last two AGM minutes and the reserve-fund balance?
  5. What are the Activity Centre, gym and pool hours, and is there any additional access charge?
  6. Is the business centre bookable, and is there a fee?
  7. What is the deposit interest arrangement, and which account is it held in?
  8. What is the total once-off cost — lease admin, credit check, access tags, meter activation?
  9. Does the complex have a generator or inverter for load-shedding, and water tanks for Joburg Water outages?
  10. What is the early-cancellation penalty, and can it be capped in writing?
  11. Is the R10,000 deposit confirmed for the unfurnished option?
Petra Nera
  1. Does the R800 include sanitation, sewerage and refuse — or are those billed separately?
  2. What is the deposit and the total once-off move-in cost?
  3. What is the annual escalation percentage?
  4. What is the security setup — guard, access control, CCTV, perimeter?
  5. How many parking bays, and are they covered or allocated?
  6. Backup power and water storage during load-shedding and municipal outages?
  7. Age and condition of the geyser, plumbing and roof — and who pays for repairs?
  8. Will the landlord contribute to repainting or basic cosmetic improvement given the condition?
  9. Can I sub-let the third bedroom, and is that permitted in the lease?
  10. What is the exact walking route and distance to the CRC entrance, and is it lit at night?
