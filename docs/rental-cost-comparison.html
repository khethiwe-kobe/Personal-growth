Petra Nera — two things to press on

The R2,000 admin fee is high. Market rate for a lease initiation fee is R950–R1,500, so this is roughly R500–R1,000 above normal and it is non-refundable. Worth asking what it covers, and whether it is negotiable.

The utilities came in at R1,150, not R800 — R1,000 electricity and R150 water. That is R350 a month more than first quoted, and it checks out: a three-bedroom heating its own water at City Power’s 2026/27 rates should land near R1,000, and it is exactly R300 above SoHo’s two-bed on a communal geyser. Both figures now corroborate each other. The gap has narrowed from R1,330 to R980.

What is still unknown is sanitation and refuse. SoHo bills these separately at R900 and R500. If Petra Nera does the same, that is up to R1,400 a month on top of the R1,150 — which would make SoHo Junction the cheaper flat outright. This is now the single most valuable question left to ask, and it is worth more than the rent difference, the gym and the Uber fares combined.

Verdict

Petra Nera — on a margin now thin enough to be uncomfortable

Petra Nera is R980 a month cheaper — R490 each. It gives the two of you a third room to use as a study and removes the church commute entirely. On the numbers as they now stand, it still wins.

But the margin has halved twice. It was R1,672 on a private car with the original utility quote; R1,330 once Uber replaced the car; and R980 now that Petra Nera’s real utility bill came in R350 higher. Two forces did that. The gym at Petra Nera costs R1,820 a month once Uber is counted, which cancels almost all of the walking-distance saving. And Petra Nera pays for its own geyser where SoHo does not.

R980 is roughly the value of one unanswered question. If sanitation and refuse are billed on top at Petra Nera the way they are at SoHo, the decision reverses.

Three questions decide it

Q1

Does Petra Nera bill sanitation and refuse on top of the R1,150? SoHo charges R900 and R500 for exactly these. Anything above R980 on that line and SoHo Junction is the cheaper flat. Get it in writing before anything else — this single answer outweighs the rent, the gym and every fare in this document.

Q2

How seriously will you train? At two visits a week Petra Nera is R1,370 ahead. At 5.5 the two are dead even; at six, SoHo is cheaper. That crossover used to be out of reach — it no longer is. If either of you trains properly, SoHo’s gym is not a perk, it is a line item worth R1,820 a month.

Q3

Is R490 each enough to buy the flat you like less? That is what the decision now costs. R490 a month is real money, but it is no longer the kind of gap that settles an argument on its own — and Petra Nera is the plainer place to come home to. Set against it: nothing stands between you and church. No booking, no fare, no waiting. That is the strongest thing Petra Nera has, and it is not on this spreadsheet.

Recommendation: Petra Nera — conditional on confirming in writing that sanitation and refuse are not billed on top, seeing the security setup in person, and walking the Riverbend Road route to CRC after dark yourselves. But this is now a close call rather than a clear one. If that levy question comes back at more than about R980, or if you both intend to train five or six days a week, choose SoHo Junction — on those numbers it is both cheaper and materially better to live in.

Method

How the utility figures were checked

Water — R50 confirmed

Johannesburg Water gives the first 6 kL a month free to residential customers, then charges R16.45/kL from 6 to 10 kL. Two people typically use 6–8 kL, so the metered cost really is in the tens of rands. R50 is right — the earlier R350 estimate was wrong. Watch only for the demand-management levy, which rose to about R108 a month in July 2026 and may sit on the body-corporate account rather than yours.

Electricity — R120 is the fixed charge, not the bill

City Power’s 2026/27 prepaid residential tariff runs R3.06–R4.37 per kWh, plus roughly R200 a month in fixed charges — a R70 service charge and a R130 network capacity charge. That R130 is almost certainly the R120 figure you had in mind. Actual consumption sits on top of it. A two-person flat uses 300–450 kWh a month; strip out the geyser at 30–40% and SoHo lands around 200–280 kWh, or R650–R900. R700 is used here. Petra Nera’s actual R1,000 — a larger flat, heating its own water — sits almost exactly where this model predicts, which is good evidence the R700 estimate for SoHo is sound. Still worth asking the agent for a current tenant’s real number.
