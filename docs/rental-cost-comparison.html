Why SoHo’s house is cheaper

Two reasons, both structural. The R8,800 covers sanitation, refuse and security, which the other SoHo unit bills at R1,400 on top. And electricity runs R380 lower because the geyser sits on the body corporate’s account and the hobs are gas — Petra Nera heats its own water. Rent is nearly identical; everything under it is not.

Who pays it

The Wits cost is R617 each at both places — R206 for the two shared Wednesdays, R411 for the two you travel separately. Identical either way.

The CRC R1,300 falls on one person. That is the entire difference between the two flats, carried by whoever makes the church run.

A note on the month

These use 4.33 weeks — the true yearly average, not a 4-week month. Your “two together, two apart” pattern is applied as a 50/50 split, so 2.17 Wednesdays each way. A clean 4-Wednesday month would read R380 shared and R760 separate, about 8% lower than shown.

The one question that would settle water

SoHo bills water through STSS prepaid sub-metering, so the rate you pay is set by how the body corporate passes on Johannesburg Water’s stepped tariff. If it passes the free first 6 kL through per unit, two people would pay almost nothing. If it reconciles the whole scheme in bulk, you pay for every kilolitre. That is the difference between R20 and R250 a month — ask what the STSS rate per kilolitre is.

Electricity is firmer: the tariff is published, the fittings are confirmed, and Petra Nera’s actual R1,000 for a larger flat heating its own water sits exactly where this model predicts. R620 is sound to within about R150 either way.

Still one question outstanding

SoHo has confirmed what its rent covers. Petra Nera has not. If its R1,150 excludes sanitation and refuse the way SoHo’s R9,500 unit does, the R720 becomes a R680 deficit and SoHo wins. That single answer is worth more than every other line on this page.

Questions to ask

Petra Nera
  1. Does the R1,150 cover sanitation, sewerage and refuse, or are those billed on top? Worth R680 either way — this decides it.
  2. If billed separately, what did they come to last month?
  3. Can we see a recent utility bill, so the R1,150 is confirmed rather than quoted?
  4. What does the R2,000 admin fee cover, and is it negotiable? Market is R950–R1,500.
  5. What is the annual escalation percentage?
  6. Security setup — guard, access control, CCTV, perimeter?
  7. How many parking bays, covered or allocated?
  8. Backup power and water storage during outages?
  9. Age of geyser, plumbing and roof — who pays for repairs?
  10. Will the landlord contribute to repainting?
  11. Is the walking route to CRC lit at night?
  12. Availability, and is R9,000 held for a 12-month lease?
SoHo Junction R8,800
  1. Get the levy inclusion written into the lease — that sanitation, refuse and security are covered by the R8,800 and cannot be billed separately later.
  2. What is the STSS rate per kilolitre for water? The difference between the free 6 kL being passed through or not is R20 against R250 a month.
  3. What does a two-bed here actually spend on prepaid electricity? Ask a current tenant or the estate manager.
  4. What is the floor size? The other 2-bed 2-bath in the complex is 62 m².
  5. If the body corporate raises the levies mid-lease, who absorbs it?
  6. Is the carport allocated, and can a second bay be rented? At what cost?
  7. What is the annual escalation percentage?
  8. Any special levies planned? Can we see the last two AGM minutes?
  9. Total once-off cost — admin, credit checks for two, tags, meter activation?
  10. Deposit interest arrangement, and which account?
  11. Generator or inverter for load-shedding, and water tanks for outages?
  12. Early-cancellation penalty — can it be capped in writing?
