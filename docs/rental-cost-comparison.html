Where Telford’s rent saving goes

The R7,980 unit is R820 a month cheaper in rent than SoHo. But SoHo’s geyser sits on the body corporate’s account and its hobs are gas, so its electricity runs about R580 lower — and its water is free inside 6 kL where Telford’s is unknown. By the time you reach the house total the R820 advantage has shrunk to R90.

The asymmetry that matters

SoHo has answered the levy question; Telford has not. Neither Telford listing states levies, rates, sanitation, refuse or utilities — and both are let by managing agents on behalf of individual landlords, where billing sanitation and refuse to the tenant is common. At SoHo’s own rate that is R1,290–R1,400 a month.

Three of the five rows above put SoHo ahead, and all three turn on charges Telford has not disclosed. That is not a reason to dismiss Telford — it is a reason to make one phone call before deciding.

What Telford genuinely wins on

Space. 90–95 m² against roughly 62 is not a rounding difference — it is a materially bigger flat, and at R137–139 per m² against R202 it is about a third better value per square metre. The R8,500 unit also has a garage plus two bays where SoHo has one carport.

It is also on Witkoppen Road, a straight run to CRC with no cross-suburb navigation, and both units have renovated bathrooms and a main en-suite.

Table 6

What to ask Telford

Four questions settle this. The first is worth more than the other three together.

Telford Court — both agents
  1. Does the rent cover sanitation, sewerage, refuse and the security levy — or are those billed on top? Worth up to R1,300 a month. SoHo includes them; if Telford does not, SoHo is cheaper by R662–R1,262 and the comparison is over.
  2. Is the geyser electric, gas or solar, and is the stove gas or electric? The R580 electricity gap between these flats is almost entirely this. A gas stove or solar geyser at Telford would put it R338 ahead of SoHo instead of level.
  3. Is water metered per unit, and does the free first 6 kL reach the meter?
  4. What is the deposit, the admin fee, and the annual escalation?
  5. Is there backup power or water storage? SoHo has none confirmed either — worth asking both.
  6. How old is the complex, and when were the geyser, plumbing and roof last done? Tennis and squash courts suggest 1980s–90s stock, which usually means more space and more maintenance.
  7. Confirm the exact street address for the R7,980 unit — the listing withholds the number.
