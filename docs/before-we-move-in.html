Part one · On your own

Decide your position before you walk in

You cannot negotiate a position you have not decided. Work through this alone first — not to build a case, but so that when a question comes up you answer from what you actually want rather than from what is easiest to agree to in the moment.

What you can and cannot absorb

From month four your share settles at about R5,430 a month including transport, against R12,000 available. That is comfortable. The thin part is the first month, and the thing that keeps it safe is the deposit being split over three instalments.

Prepare for this one

If your pastor earns considerably more than you, they may propose things in good faith that you cannot afford — furniture, a weekly cleaner, splitting something large. Decide now that you will say "that is above what I budgeted" plainly, and without explaining your finances. It is a complete sentence. Rehearse it, because the moment it is needed is the moment it will be hardest to say.

04People in the home

The line already drawn

The flat is not a ministry venue. Church members do not come here. That is settled, and it is a good line — a pastor's home otherwise tends to become semi-public without anyone deciding that it should. This part of the conversation is not about negotiating it. It is about making sure it holds without either of you having to defend it at the door.

07If something changes

The item both of you will want to skip. Do it anyway — and do it now, while everyone is glad about the flat, because it cannot be done well later.

The conflict route

When something goes wrong between flatmates, most people talk to someone they trust. Your usual person is the other flatmate. That route is closed here, and closing it without noticing is the specific risk of this arrangement.

If one of you wants out

If the relationship changes

You have said there is more between you than a shared address. That is yours, and none of what follows is a judgement on it. But you asked not to be surprised, so three things are worth saying plainly.

The lease will outlive any change between you. If things end in month three, you are still contractually bound to each other for the rest of the term. Decide now, while it costs nothing, who would move out and on what notice.

A relationship between a pastor and a member of their congregation carries professional exposure in most churches — often more for them than for you. Whatever you have both decided about that, decide it together and decide it before you sign, not in the middle of a difficult conversation with someone else.

The imbalance is real even when the affection is. They hold spiritual authority over you and, once you sign, a shared financial obligation with you. That does not make the relationship wrong. It does mean you should keep your own money, your own exit, and your own outside person — not because you expect to need them, but because having them is what lets you stay in this freely.

If this changed between us, what would we do about the flat — and can we agree that now, in writing, while it is easy?

Part three · On paper

Write down the six that matter

Not a contract, and not a sign of distrust. It exists because memory is unreliable and because the friendship is worth protecting from an argument about who paid for the couch. One page, both signatures, a copy each.

Flatmate agreement

  1. Money. Rent is split ___ / ___. Electricity, internet and consumables are split ___ / ___. Payment is made ___ by the ___ of each month.
  2. Deposit. Each paid R4,400. On exit it is refunded in the same proportion, less any damage caused by the person responsible for it.
  3. Notice. Either of us gives the other ___ days' notice before moving out. The person leaving finds a replacement, subject to the other's agreement.
  4. Guests. ___ hours' notice. Overnight guests: ___. The flat is not used for ministry and church members do not visit, except ______________.
  5. Joint spending. Neither commits the other to anything over R___ without agreement.
  6. Disputes. Anything we cannot settle between us goes to ___________, whom we both accept. Household decisions are made as equals.

Most of this will never be needed. You will settle into a rhythm, and the page will sit in a drawer. That is the point of it — the arrangements that last are the ones where nobody has to guess what was agreed, and where both people kept the freedom to change their mind.
